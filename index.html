02 — WATER QUALITY

What's inside your glass?

Safe drinking water is assessed using physical, chemical and microbiological characteristics. There is no single number that defines every aspect of water safety.

WATER QUALITY

Know what to look for

SAFETY CHECK
H₂O +
pH Often assessed around a near-neutral range
Turbidity Lower cloudiness is generally preferred
Fluoride WHO guideline value: 1.5 mg/L
Nitrate WHO guideline value: 50 mg/L as nitrate
ⓘ

These values are examples of important parameters, not a complete water-testing checklist. Actual standards and testing requirements depend on the water source and applicable regulations.

PH
ACIDITY / ALKALINITY pH

pH indicates how acidic or alkaline water is. It is one of several parameters used in water-quality assessment.

TDS
DISSOLVED MATERIAL TDS

Total dissolved solids influence taste and acceptability. WHO does not set a health-based guideline value for TDS.

NTU
CLARITY Turbidity

Turbidity describes cloudiness caused by suspended particles and can indicate problems requiring investigation.

μg
CHEMICAL SAFETY Contaminants

Substances such as lead, arsenic, nitrate and fluoride require appropriate monitoring.

03 — THE CHALLENGE

Water can become unsafe
after the tap.

Water safety is not only about the original source. The container, hands, taps, surroundings and habits can all affect hygiene.

01
🚰

Unclean taps & filling areas

Frequently touched surfaces around water points can become dirty when cleaning and maintenance are neglected.

HYGIENE
02
🧴

Dirty bottles

Reusing a bottle without regularly cleaning it can allow residue and microorganisms to accumulate.

CONTAINERS
03
🖐️

Dirty hands

Touching bottle openings or drinking surfaces with unwashed hands can introduce contamination.

PERSONAL HYGIENE
04
👥

Sharing bottles

Sharing drinking containers increases direct contact between people and their saliva.

CROSS-CONTACT
05
🌡️

Limited or unreliable supply

Water scarcity, infrastructure gaps, pollution, climate pressures and uneven access can make safe water difficult to obtain consistently.

GLOBAL CHALLENGE
04 — HEALTH & DISEASE

Unsafe water can carry serious consequences.

Microbiologically contaminated drinking water can transmit several diseases. Good water, sanitation and hygiene practices are important tools for reducing these risks.

🦠 BACTERIAL

Cholera

A bacterial infection associated with contaminated food or water that can cause severe diarrhoeal illness.

🧫 BACTERIAL

Typhoid

An infection caused by Salmonella Typhi and commonly spread through contaminated food or water.

💧 WATERBORNE

Diarrhoeal diseases

Contaminated water can contribute to diarrhoeal illness, especially where sanitation and hygiene are inadequate.

⚠️ INFECTION

Dysentery

Certain infections causing diarrhoea and intestinal inflammation can spread through contaminated water.

!
Remember

Water that looks clear is not automatically free from microorganisms or chemicals. Visual appearance alone cannot establish drinking-water safety.

05 — WHAT WE CAN DO

Small habits.
Big protection.

Water safety isn't only the responsibility of governments, laboratories or water suppliers. Everyday choices also help protect the water we consume.

YOUR WATER SAFETY CHECKLIST 0 / 8
🧴
01

Clean containers

Wash reusable bottles regularly and keep drinking surfaces clean.

🧼
02

Clean hands

Hand hygiene helps reduce the transfer of microorganisms to drinking surfaces.

🚰
03

Protect water points

Keep taps and surrounding filling areas clean and report problems when you notice them.

♨️
04

Treat when appropriate

Use an appropriate treatment method when the safety of the source is uncertain.
